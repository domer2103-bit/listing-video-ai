# How Long Should a Property Listing Video Be?

The honest answer is "it depends where it's being watched" — but that's not a satisfying place to stop, so here's the actual breakdown by platform, property size, and what the data suggests about where attention drops off.

## The general rule: 60–120 seconds for a full walkthrough

For a standard residential listing embedded on a portal or an agency website, the sweet spot is **60 to 120 seconds**. That's long enough to cover every main room without rushing, and short enough that a buyer watches to the end rather than clicking away partway through.

Below 45 seconds, a video starts to feel like a trailer rather than a walkthrough — fine as a teaser, but not enough to replace the job photos are doing. Above 2.5 minutes, engagement drops sharply; most viewers, even genuinely interested buyers, won't sit through a three-minute tour of a three-bedroom semi.

## How property size changes the math

The right length scales with the property, but not linearly:

- **1–2 bedroom flats:** 45–75 seconds is usually plenty. Fewer rooms means less ground to cover, and a longer video on a small property starts to feel padded.
- **3–4 bedroom houses:** 75–120 seconds is the standard range — enough time to give each main room a proper look plus the garden or outdoor space.
- **Larger or unique properties (5+ beds, period features, significant land):** Up to 2.5–3 minutes can work, but only if every additional second is earning its place. A grand staircase or a substantial garden justifies extra time; a fourth generic bedroom doesn't.

## Platform changes the ceiling, not just the floor

Where the video will actually be watched matters as much as the property itself:

**Portal listings (Rightmove, Zoopla, Airbnb):** Buyers here are often mid-scroll, comparing multiple properties. Aim for the lower end of your range — a tight, well-paced video outperforms a longer one when it's competing for attention against a dozen other open tabs.

**Your own website or a direct email to a warm lead:** You can go slightly longer, since the viewer has already shown intent by clicking through specifically. Up to 3 minutes is reasonable here.

**Social media (Instagram, TikTok, Reels):** Cut it down hard — 15 to 30 seconds, leading with the single strongest feature. Social platforms reward videos that hook attention in the first two seconds and don't need to cover every room; the goal there is to drive a click to the full listing, not to replace it.

## Pacing matters more than total length

A 90-second video that's well-paced will outperform a 60-second video that rushes. A few pacing principles that hold regardless of total length:

- **Give the strongest room the most time**, not the largest room. A modest but beautifully renovated kitchen deserves more screen time than a big but unremarkable bedroom.
- **Vary the shot duration.** A video where every room gets exactly the same number of seconds feels mechanical. Let the property's actual highlights set the rhythm.
- **Front-load the best material.** The first 10–15 seconds decide whether a scrolling viewer keeps watching — this is not where to put the boring hallway shot.
- **End on something that closes the deal**, not the utility room. A garden, a view, or an establishing shot of the location works better as a closer than wherever the photo set happened to end.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a longer video ever better for a luxury listing?**
Sometimes, but the same rules about earning the extra time apply. A luxury property with genuinely distinct spaces (a wine cellar, a home cinema, extensive grounds) can justify 3 minutes or more. A luxury property that's just a bigger version of a standard layout doesn't automatically need more time.

**Should the video length match how many photos I have?**
No — length should match how much is worth showing, not how many photos exist. It's fine to skip a filler photo in the video even if it's in the portal gallery.

**Do shorter videos really perform better, or is that assumption outdated?**
The data still supports it, particularly on mobile and on portals where a buyer is comparing multiple listings in one session. Attention is the scarce resource; shorter, well-paced videos protect it better than longer ones.

## The bottom line

For most listings, 60 to 120 seconds is the right target — shorter for flats and social cuts, longer only when a property genuinely has more worth showing. Pacing that front-loads the strongest material matters more than hitting an exact runtime.
