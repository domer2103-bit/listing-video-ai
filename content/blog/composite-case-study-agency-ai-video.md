# An Illustrative Scenario: How an Agency Might Use AI Video to Win More Listings

A note before this one starts: this isn't a report on a real, named agency — it's a composite, illustrative scenario built from the kinds of workflows and outcomes described elsewhere on this blog, put together to make the practical application concrete. Treat it as a worked example, not a case study of an actual business.

## The scenario

Picture a small independent estate agency — three agents, roughly 15 to 25 active listings at any time — competing in a local market against larger agencies with bigger marketing budgets. Their existing process: professional photos for every listing (a fixed cost they already budget for), a written description, and no video, because video previously meant either an additional videographer cost per listing or asking an already-stretched agent to learn video editing on top of everything else.

## The problem this kind of agency typically faces

Smaller agencies competing against larger ones often lose on marketing polish rather than on service quality — a buyer comparing two similar properties from different agencies may gravitate toward the one with the more complete-feeling listing, even if the smaller agency's actual service is just as good or better. Video is increasingly part of what "complete-feeling" means to buyers who've come to expect it from experience with the largest platforms and agencies.

## How this kind of agency could realistically adopt AI video

**Step 1 — starting with existing listings.** Rather than treating video as something to add only to new listings going forward, an agency in this position could reasonably start by generating video for every currently active listing using the photos already on file — since the photos already exist, this is largely a one-time catch-up effort rather than an ongoing new cost per listing.

**Step 2 — folding it into the standard process.** Going forward, generating a video becomes a standard step alongside photography for every new listing, taking a few minutes per property rather than requiring a separate booking or specialist.

**Step 3 — repurposing across channels.** The same video used on the portal listing also gets shared on the agency's social channels and included in follow-up messages to interested buyers — getting more use out of each video than a single listing page placement would.

## Plausible outcomes, reasoned from what's established elsewhere

Based on the patterns described throughout this blog — video improving inquiry rates, reducing viewing no-shows through better-set expectations, and supporting review and reputation quality over time — an agency following this approach could reasonably expect a meaningful improvement in listing engagement and buyer confidence relative to their photo-only baseline, without the video line item straining a small agency's marketing budget the way hiring a dedicated videographer for every listing would.

It's worth being honest that the specific magnitude of any such improvement would vary by market, property mix, and execution — this scenario illustrates a plausible pattern, not a guaranteed specific percentage outcome.

## What made this hypothetical work

The core enabler in this scenario isn't a marketing trick — it's that photo-based AI video removes the two barriers (specialist cost, and time/skill investment) that most realistically stop smaller agencies from adopting video: no photographer skill required beyond what they already have for stills, and no per-listing videographer fee eating into thin agency margins.
