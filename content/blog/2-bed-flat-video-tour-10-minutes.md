# How to Turn a 2-Bed Flat Listing Into a Video Tour in 10 Minutes

To make this concrete rather than abstract, here's exactly what the process looks like for a typical listing — a 2-bedroom flat with a fairly standard photo set — from opening the tool to having a finished video ready to publish.

## Minute 0–1: Starting point

Assume a fairly ordinary set of listing photos: living room, kitchen, two bedrooms, bathroom, and maybe an exterior shot — six to eight images total, taken on a phone or by an estate agent, reasonably well lit but not professionally staged. This is the photo set most flats already have, not a special one assembled for the purpose.

## Minute 1–2: Uploading or linking

If the flat is already listed somewhere with photos published — Rightmove, Zoopla, an agent's site — the fastest starting point is often pasting that listing URL directly into a tool like [Online Viewing](https://www.onlineviewing.co.uk), which pulls the existing photos automatically. Otherwise, uploading the photo set directly from a folder takes about the same amount of time.

## Minute 2–4: Filling in the details

A short intake covers what the photos alone don't convey:

- **Property type and location** — "2-bed flat, [area]" gives useful context for the narration.
- **Key facts worth mentioning** — square footage if known, any recent updates (new kitchen, recent redecoration), proximity to transport links.
- **Labelling photos**, if the order or room identity isn't obvious from the images alone — this matters more for open-plan spaces.
- **Narration voice**, if there's a choice on offer.

This is the only part of the process that requires active input, and it's genuinely just filling in a short form, not writing a script from scratch.

## Minute 4–6: Generation

This is the part with no manual work required. The tool sequences the photos into a logical walkthrough order, applies appropriate motion to each image, generates narration from the details provided, and scores the whole thing with pacing and (usually) background music. For a flat this size, this step typically takes somewhere in the region of two to three minutes.

## Minute 6–8: Review

Watch the finished video once, checking specifically for:

- Room order that matches how someone would actually walk through the flat
- Any mislabelled space (a second bedroom described as an office, for instance)
- Narration that sounds natural and matches what's on screen
- Pacing that feels proportionate — not lingering too long on a small space, not rushing past the kitchen if it's a genuine selling point

## Minute 8–10: Adjustments and publishing

If anything needs fixing, most tools let you adjust a detail and regenerate quickly rather than starting the whole process over — this is usually the fastest part of the whole workflow if a change is needed at all. Once you're satisfied, download the finished video and add it to wherever the listing lives — a portal listing, a social post, or both.

## What makes this realistic in 10 minutes and not longer

The time compression comes from two things: reusing photos that already exist rather than scheduling a new shoot, and the tool handling sequencing, motion, narration and pacing automatically rather than requiring manual video editing. Neither of those steps has historically been fast — a filmed and edited walkthrough realistically takes days between scheduling, shooting and editing. Removing both bottlenecks is what gets the whole thing down to single-digit minutes of active time.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does this process change for a larger property, like a 4-bed house?**
The steps are the same; generation time scales modestly with more photos and rooms, but the active work — uploading, filling in details, reviewing — stays roughly similar regardless of property size.

**What if my photos aren't in a logical room order to begin with?**
Most tools sequence photos into a sensible walkthrough order automatically rather than requiring them to be pre-sorted, though labelling ambiguous rooms helps get this right the first time.

**Can I generate a second, shorter cut for social media from the same photos?**
Yes, in most tools — a shorter cut for Instagram or TikTok is usually a quick variant of the same generation rather than a separate project.
