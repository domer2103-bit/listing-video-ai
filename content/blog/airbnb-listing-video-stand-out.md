# How to Make Your Airbnb Listing Stand Out With Video

Search Airbnb for almost any city and you'll get back a grid of listings that, at thumbnail size, look remarkably similar — a bright living room, a made bed, a clean kitchen counter. Every host has read the same photography advice, and it shows. Video is one of the few remaining ways to actually differentiate, because most listings still don't have one.

## Why photos alone struggle to differentiate anymore

Airbnb's own photo guidance has become widely known and widely followed — bright natural light, decluttered surfaces, wide-angle shots of the main living space. That's good advice, and it's raised the average listing's photo quality across the board. The downside is that it's also made listings look more alike, not less. When every host follows the same photography playbook, photos stop being a differentiator and become table stakes.

Video hasn't reached the same saturation point. Most listings still don't have one, which means a listing that does stands out simply by having something most competitors don't.

## What video actually adds for a guest

Guests booking a short-term stay are making a faster, more emotional decision than a homebuyer — often comparing several listings side by side in one sitting, deciding in minutes rather than weeks. Video helps in a few specific ways that matter for that faster decision:

**It answers "does this actually feel nice to be in?"** more convincingly than photos. Motion and narration give a sense of atmosphere that a static photo grid can't fully capture.

**It builds trust faster.** A listing that clearly shows every space in sequence — not just the flattering angles — reads as more transparent, which matters more for short-term bookings where a guest is often paying in full upfront, sight unseen.

**It sets expectations accurately**, which reduces the gap between what a guest expected and what they find on arrival — one of the most common sources of a bad review that otherwise has nothing to do with the property itself.

## What to include in an Airbnb walkthrough video

Unlike a real estate sales listing, an Airbnb video should sell the *stay*, not just the space:

- **The arrival experience** — what a guest sees walking in, since first impressions matter disproportionately for short stays.
- **Sleeping arrangements clearly shown**, especially for listings that sleep more guests than the bedroom count implies (sofa beds, room configurations).
- **Anything that solves a specific traveller need** — a proper desk for remote workers, a well-equipped kitchen for longer stays, blackout curtains for shift workers.
- **The outdoor space or view, if there is one** — balconies, gardens, and views consistently drive bookings and are easy to under-represent in a standard photo set.
- **Practical context** — proximity to transport, parking if relevant, anything that reduces a guest's uncertainty about logistics.

## Where to actually use the video once you have it

- **Directly in the listing**, wherever the platform supports video in the gallery.
- **On social media**, particularly Instagram and TikTok, where short-form property content performs well and can drive direct traffic to a booking link, bypassing platform commission on that portion of bookings.
- **In pre-arrival messages to confirmed guests** — a nice touch that sets expectations and reduces day-of questions about layout or amenities.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Airbnb's own platform prioritise listings with video in search results?**
Airbnb hasn't confirmed video as a direct search ranking factor, but listings with stronger engagement (which video tends to drive) generally perform better within the platform's own ranking system.

**How long should an Airbnb video be?**
Shorter than a real estate video — 45 to 75 seconds tends to work well, since guests are typically comparing multiple listings quickly rather than doing deep research on one property.

**Is video worth it for a budget or basic listing, or just premium ones?**
It's arguably more valuable for a budget listing — at the premium end, guests are often booking on brand or amenities regardless; at the budget end, differentiation matters more because there's more direct competition on price alone.
