# 5 Types of Airbnb Video That Convert Well (and What to Copy From Each)

Rather than pointing at specific named listings — which change hosts, get delisted, or simply aren't representative once you dig in — it's more useful to look at the recurring *patterns* that show up across high-performing Airbnb videos. These are the five that come up most consistently, and what to borrow from each for your own listing.

## 1. The "arrival sequence" opener

**The pattern:** The video opens not on the living room, but on the actual sequence of arriving — the building or street exterior, the entrance, then the first interior space a guest would actually see walking in.

**What to copy:** Structure your video's opening to match the real arrival experience, not just your best room. If your listing has a nice building entrance or a distinctive front door, that's a stronger opening beat than jumping straight to the living room, because it mirrors what a guest will actually experience and builds a sense of anticipation.

## 2. The "problem-solving" walkthrough

**The pattern:** Rather than a purely aesthetic tour, the video proactively answers practical questions — showing the actual bed configuration for a listing that sleeps more guests than the bedroom count suggests, showing a working space clearly for a listing marketed at remote workers, showing parking or transport access for a location where that's a common concern.

**What to copy:** Think about the two or three questions your specific listing gets asked most often (via guest messages, or questions you'd anticipate), and make sure the video visually answers them without a guest having to ask.

## 3. The "single standout feature" video

**The pattern:** Rather than giving every room equal time, the video is structurally built around one genuinely distinctive feature — a rooftop terrace, an unusual architectural detail, a view — with everything else covered more briskly.

**What to copy:** If your listing has one feature that's genuinely uncommon for the price point or area, don't treat it as just another room in a level sequence. Give it disproportionate time and let it anchor the video's structure, even if that means other rooms get comparatively brief coverage.

## 4. The "neighbourhood context" video

**The pattern:** The video includes a beat — often near the start — that shows something of the surrounding area, not just the interior. This is especially common for listings in destinations where location is a major part of the appeal (coastal towns, city-centre apartments, listings near a specific attraction).

**What to copy:** If your listing's location is genuinely part of the pitch, don't let the video imply the property exists in a vacuum. An establishing shot or a brief mention of what's nearby in the narration does real work here — this is exactly what an [AI satellite establishing shot](/blog/ai-satellite-establishing-shot) is built for.

## 5. The "honest pacing" video

**The pattern:** Rather than lingering equally on every space regardless of size or interest, the video's pacing genuinely reflects how much there is to see — a small but charming space gets a shorter, tighter video; a larger property with more distinct areas gets proportionately more time.

**What to copy:** Resist the urge to pad a small listing's video to match the length of a bigger property's. A tight, well-paced 45-second video for a studio apartment outperforms an artificially stretched 90-second one covering the same amount of actual content.

## The common thread across all five

None of these patterns depend on production budget or equipment — they're structural and narrative choices, not technical ones. ## Frequently asked questions

**Should every video try to use all five patterns at once?**
No — pick the one or two that genuinely fit your specific property rather than forcing all five in. A studio apartment doesn't need an elaborate arrival sequence; a listing with no standout single feature shouldn't be structured around one.

**Do these patterns apply to real estate sales video too, or just Airbnb?**
Most apply to both, though the "problem-solving" pattern in particular shows up more in short-term rental video, since Airbnb guests tend to have more specific practical questions (sleeping arrangements, work space, parking) than home buyers typically ask via a listing video.

**How do I know which pattern fits my listing?**
Start from what makes your specific property different from the other listings nearby at a similar price — that's usually the pattern worth building the video around.

## The bottom line

The best-performing Airbnb videos aren't necessarily the most expensively produced — they're the ones structured around a clear pattern that fits the specific property, rather than a generic room-by-room tour.
