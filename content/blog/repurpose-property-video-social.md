# How to Repurpose Your Property Video for Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube

Once you have a finished property video, publishing it only to a listing page leaves value on the table — the same video, adapted appropriately, can work across several social platforms. Here's how to think about repurposing it well, rather than just re-uploading the same file everywhere.

## Why a single upload everywhere usually underperforms

Each platform has different norms around format, length, and pacing, and content that ignores those norms tends to underperform even when the underlying material is strong. A full-length listing walkthrough uploaded unedited to TikTok, for instance, is likely to lose viewers quickly, since TikTok's audience expects a faster opening hook than a portal listing video is built to provide.

## Instagram: Reels and feed posts

**Reels** favour a shorter cut — 15 to 30 seconds — that leads with the property's strongest single feature rather than a full room-by-room walkthrough. If your full video opens gradually, consider trimming a Reels version that starts on the standout moment instead.

**Feed posts** (static or carousel) can use individual stills pulled from the video alongside a caption, functioning more like a teaser that links to the full video in a bio link or Stories.

**Stories** work well for a quick, informal teaser clip with a "swipe up" or link sticker driving to the full listing or video.

## TikTok: fast hook, native pacing

TikTok's format rewards an even faster opening than Instagram Reels — the first one to two seconds need to earn continued attention. A property's most visually striking room or feature, shown immediately rather than built up to, tends to perform better here than the more gradual pacing that suits a portal listing page. Native-feeling captions and trending audio (where appropriate and rights-compliant) also tend to outperform a straight re-upload of listing video audio.

## YouTube: the full version, with a searchable title

YouTube is generally the best home for the full-length, unedited walkthrough — viewers arriving via search or a shared link are typically more deliberate and further along in their decision process than a casual social scroller, making a complete tour appropriate here in a way it usually isn't on TikTok or Reels. A specific, keyword-relevant title ("2-Bed Flat Tour — [Area], [Price]") helps the video surface in relevant searches.

## What to keep consistent across all versions

Even as length and pacing adapt per platform, a few things are worth keeping consistent: the property's key facts (price, location, bedroom count) should match exactly across every version, and any watermark, branding, or contact information should be present but not intrusive on every cut.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a different video for each platform, or can I trim one video into all the versions?**
Trimming and reordering one source video usually works — the main adjustment is which moment leads and how long the cut runs, not building entirely separate content.

**Should I add captions/subtitles for social versions?**
Generally yes — a large share of social video is watched with sound off, so on-screen captions meaningfully improve engagement on Instagram and TikTok specifically.

**Is it worth posting the same video natively on each platform, or linking out instead?**
Native uploads (rather than linking to another platform) generally get better reach on Instagram and TikTok specifically, since most platforms deprioritise content that sends viewers elsewhere.
