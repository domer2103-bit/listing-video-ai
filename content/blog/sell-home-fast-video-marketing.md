# Selling a Home Fast: How Video Marketing Shortens Time on Market

Every seller wants the same two things: the best price, and the shortest time on market. Those goals are usually in tension — rushing a sale tends to cost money. Video is one of the few marketing changes that genuinely supports both at once, by generating more serious interest earlier in the listing's life rather than trading price for speed.

## Why the first two weeks matter most

Most of a listing's total inquiry volume arrives in its first 10–14 days. Portal algorithms and buyer alert systems both favour freshly listed properties, and serious buyers actively watching a market tend to act quickly on new listings that match their criteria. A listing that underperforms in this early window often struggles to recover the same momentum later, even after a price adjustment.

This is exactly the window where video has the most leverage — a listing with video gets more inquiries in that critical early period, which compounds: more inquiries means more viewings booked while interest is highest, which means offers arrive while the property still has "new listing" momentum rather than after it's gone stale.

## The mechanics of how video shortens time on market

**More inquiries, earlier.** The 403% inquiry uplift video listings see isn't spread evenly over a listing's life — it's concentrated in that early high-visibility window, which is exactly when it does the most good.

**Better-qualified viewings.** Buyers who've watched a narrated walkthrough before booking a viewing arrive with a clearer sense of the property, which means fewer viewings end in "it's not what I expected" and more end in a genuine offer conversation.

**Reduced price-reduction pressure.** Listings that stall often end up cutting price to generate fresh interest — video reduces how often that becomes necessary by generating stronger interest from the outset, rather than needing a price cut to re-trigger attention later.

## A practical timeline

For sellers or agents who want video working for them from day one:

**Before listing (ideally):** Generate the video alongside your final photo set, so it's ready to publish the moment the listing goes live — not added a week in after the initial momentum has already been spent.

**If the listing is already live and stalling:** Adding video retroactively, using the existing photo set, can meaningfully revive interest without a price change. This is often a faster and cheaper first move than an immediate reduction.

**Throughout the listing period:** Reuse the same video in follow-up marketing — a price-drop announcement, a re-listing after a fallen-through sale, or a targeted email to buyers who enquired about similar properties.

## What video won't fix

Video improves marketing performance, but it doesn't change the fundamentals a buyer will eventually see in person. If a property is genuinely overpriced for its condition and location, video will generate interest faster, but it won't close a sale that the price itself is blocking. Video and pricing strategy solve different problems — treat them as complementary, not substitutes for each other.

## Frequently asked questions

**How quickly can adding video actually change inquiry volume?**
Many agents see a measurable shift within days of adding video to a listing, particularly on portals where the video badge changes the listing's appearance in search results immediately.

**Is video worth it for a property that's about to go under offer anyway?**
Probably not urgent in that case — video has the most impact on listings still actively competing for buyer attention, not ones already close to a deal.

**Does video help more with certain property types?**
It tends to help most with properties where layout and flow are a selling point — larger homes, unusual layouts, or anything where photos alone struggle to convey how the spaces connect.
