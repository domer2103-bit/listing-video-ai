# Airbnb Photography vs Video: What Actually Books More Nights

Hosts weighing where to spend limited time and budget often frame this as a choice: invest in better photography, or add video. It's worth being clear that these aren't really competing investments — they do different jobs, and the listings that book the most nights tend to have both, not one instead of the other.

## What photography does that video can't replace

Photos are still the foundation. They're what appears in search thumbnails, what guests scan through first, and what most platforms treat as the primary gallery content. Good photography — bright natural light, wide-angle shots that show full rooms, clean and decluttered spaces — remains the single highest-leverage thing a host can control about how a listing looks.

Video is generated *from* photos in most modern tools, including [Online Viewing](https://www.onlineviewing.co.uk) — which means photo quality is actually a prerequisite for good video, not an alternative to it. A video built from poor photos will still look like it was built from poor photos, just in motion.

## What video adds that photography can't

Where photography shows individual spaces, video shows how they connect and what it feels like to move through them. A guest looking at photos has to mentally assemble a floor plan from separate images; a video does that assembly for them.

Video also communicates things photography structurally can't: pacing, flow, a sense of scale that's hard to convey in a single still frame, and narration that can add context no photo caption typically includes ("the sofa bed here sleeps two additional guests" is much clearer said aloud over the relevant shot than buried in a text description).

## The data on combining both

Listings that use video alongside strong photography consistently outperform listings using either alone. This tracks with how guests actually use a listing page — photos do the initial scanning and comparison work, and video does the deeper "am I sure about this one" evaluation once a listing has made it into a guest's shortlist. Skipping either step in that sequence loses some guests along the way.

## Where to prioritise if budget or time is genuinely limited

If a host truly has to choose one improvement right now, the answer generally depends on where the current listing is weakest:

**If your photos are outdated, dark, or inconsistent:** Fix photography first. Video generated from bad source photos won't outperform a listing with strong photos and no video at all.

**If your photos are already solid but the listing still isn't converting well:** Video is likely the higher-leverage next step, since you're not bottlenecked on source material — you just haven't given guests the fuller picture video provides.

**If you're starting from scratch with a new listing:** Do both at once. Since video tools generate directly from your photo set, there's no reason to stagger them — a same-day photo shoot followed immediately by video generation gets you both improvements before the listing even goes live.

## The practical cost comparison

Professional Airbnb photography typically costs somewhere between £100 and £300 depending on property size and location, as a one-off cost that's worth repeating whenever the space changes meaningfully. AI-generated video, by contrast, is usually either free or a low monthly cost covering multiple properties — meaning it's not really competing with photography for the same budget line. Most hosts can afford both without it being a meaningful trade-off.

## Frequently asked questions

**If I only have phone photos, is video still worth adding?**
Yes, though the result will reflect the photo quality. It's still likely to outperform phone photos alone, even if professional photography plus video would perform better still.

**Do I need to redo my video every time I update photos?**
Generally yes, if the photo update is significant — a video built from an old photo set will show an outdated version of the space, which risks the same expectation-mismatch problem unedited listings already have.

**Does video replace the need for a floor plan?**
Not entirely — some guests specifically look for a floor plan to understand layout in a format they can study at their own pace. Video and a floor plan serve slightly different, complementary purposes.

## The bottom line

Photography and video aren't rival investments competing for the same budget — they're sequential ones that build on each other, with video typically generated directly from the photo set a host already has. The listings that book the most nights use both, in that order, rather than treating one as a substitute for the other.
