# Why Listings With Video Get More Buyer Inquiries

If you've listed a property in the last few years, you already know the routine: a dozen or so photos, a floor plan, a paragraph of copy, and then you wait. Most agents and sellers still treat video as optional — a nice-to-have if there's time and budget left over. The data says that's backwards.

Listings with video get up to **403% more buyer inquiries** than listings with photos alone. That's not a marginal lift. That's the difference between a listing that sits quietly on page two of search results and one that generates real momentum in its first 48 hours.

## Why the gap is so large

It comes down to how people actually browse property portals. A buyer scrolling Rightmove, Zoopla, or Airbnb at 9pm on their phone is moving fast — skimming thumbnails, not reading descriptions. Photos are static information: they tell you what a room looks like, but not what it *feels* like to move through it. Video closes that gap in seconds.

There's also a simple attention mechanic at play. A thumbnail with a play button and a hint of motion interrupts the scroll in a way a static photo grid doesn't. Portals and social platforms increasingly favour video in their own ranking and recommendation logic too, which means a video-enabled listing isn't just more persuasive once someone clicks — it's more likely to get clicked in the first place.

## It's not just about "more" — it's about *better* inquiries

More inquiries is good. But agents who've switched to video also report a shift in the *quality* of inquiries. A buyer who has already watched a two-minute walkthrough has effectively pre-qualified themselves — they know the layout, the light, the flow between rooms. The questions that come in after watching a video tend to be further down the funnel: "Is the loft boarded?" rather than "Can you send more photos of the kitchen?"

That matters for time-on-market too. Fewer wasted viewings with buyers who were never really a fit means agents spend their time on people who are genuinely close to making an offer.

## Why most sellers still skip it

If video works this well, why isn't every listing using it? Historically, three reasons:

1. **Cost.** A videographer for a single listing can run into hundreds of pounds, which doesn't make sense for a £250,000 flat with a six-week shelf life.
2. **Time.** Coordinating a film crew around a seller's schedule, good light, and a tidy property adds days to a listing timeline agents don't have.
3. **Skill.** Editing raw footage into something polished — cutting, colour grading, adding narration — is a real skill most agents don't have and don't want to learn.

Those three blockers are exactly what AI-narrated video tools are built to remove.

## What to do with the extra inquiries

Getting more inquiries only helps if you're set up to handle them well. A few practical habits worth pairing with video:

- **Respond fast.** Video-driven inquiries tend to arrive in a burst right after the listing goes live — have a plan for same-day replies in that first 48-hour window.
- **Link the video everywhere**, not just the portal listing — your own site, social posts, and follow-up emails to your buyer database.
- **Track it.** If your CRM or portal analytics let you separate video views from photo views, do it. The uplift is easy to prove to a seller once you have your own numbers next to the 403% headline figure.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does video replace professional photography?**
No — good photos are still the raw material a video is built from. Video adds motion and narration on top of strong photography, it doesn't replace the need for well-lit, well-composed shots.

Not with an AI-narrated tool. You provide the photos (or a listing link); the tool handles the animation, pacing, and voiceover.
